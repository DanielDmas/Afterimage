# AFTERIMAGE — Story Bible
**Document type:** Narrative canon + writing reference. Companion to `master_plan.md` (§2) — spoiler-complete, internal only.
**Purpose:** every lie in this game must be authored against a known truth (§6.2 discipline). This file is that truth: fixed timeline, character sheets with voice, place gazetteer, and ground-truth outlines for the prologue + slice missions. Full per-mission ground-truth documents (minute-timeline, every NPC's knowledge) deepen from the outlines here during each mission's authoring pass — but nothing written later may contradict this file without a written amendment.

## 1. Fixed Canon Timeline

### 1993 — The Meridian Affair (backstory, immutable)
- **Spring 1993:** Banka Meridian collapses under unsecured loans to Zálesí Strojírny, asset-stripped by its own management in concert with deputy director **Pavel Rys**. Argus — then a small "security services" outfit under **Zdeněk Hora** — handles the affair's dirty work.
- **Three weeks before the collapse goes public:** junior accountant **Ivana Šebestová**, the whistleblower, dies by suicide — induced by targeted Argus blackmail using her family's StB-era file. The file work was done by **Josef Sedlák**. This is Argus's founding sin and the archive's oldest secret.
- **Autumn 1993:** a forensic audit touches Argus once — and is buried. (Inside fiction, the auditor's report exists in a ministry archive; nobody who matters ever read it. One optional late-game document; players of nothing need players of nothing.)
- **1994:** director **Antonín Karas**, negligent but not the architect, is convicted — the tabloid version becomes official history. Rys emigrates (Vienna, then nothing). Argus is never charged.

### 1994–2003 — The Metastasis (interim, immutable)
- Argus converts blackmail craft into three businesses: corporate security (legal), the private archive (StB files + a decade of wiretaps; profoundly illegal), enforcement (quiet). Hora's rule: *never sell the archive, only rent conclusions from it* — sourceless answers, so the asset never leaves the building.
- **1999:** Kamila Rohanová hired (ex-military intelligence analyst); builds the archive into a modern operation. **2001:** Standa Vrba, with Hora since the early days, formally takes enforcement. The two never fight in front of the staff. Everyone knows anyway.
- **2003, December:** Hora's diagnosis (pancreatic, terminal, undisclosed). He tells no one; he starts *tidying* — which perceptive people misread as consolidating power.

### 2004 — The Game (12 missions, ~14 weeks, February–May)
- **February:** police operation VRANÁ inserts Eliška as "Radek Mráz" (Prologue; missions 1–2). The operation's stated goal: map the archive for a prosecutable seizure. Doubek's unstated goal: a case big enough to survive the politics.
- **March:** Radek proves useful; the succession war begins courting him (missions 3–6). Hora's tidying accelerates.
- **April:** Eliška finds her own recruitment file in the archive (mission 5 aftermath → missions 7–9); the mole hunt runs both directions.
- **Late April:** Hora dies (mission 10). The succession detonates (mission 11).
- **May 1, 2004 — EU accession day:** the finale (mission 12) plays against the city's accession celebrations: fireworks over Vranov while the archive's fate — and Eliška's — is decided. The new Europe arrives either way; the question is what gets carried into it.

### The mole (spoiler, immutable)
The leak feeding Argus from inside the police is **not Doubek** — it is a records officer in the regional command whose 1980s file Sedlák has held for twenty years: coerced, small, sad — a preview of what the archive does to people, and a mirror for Eliška (the archive holds *her* file now too). The red-herring pool (master_plan §2.4) is authored so the tabloid-shaped suspect is always more satisfying than the truth. House style.

## 2. Character Sheets (want · mask · voice · sample line)
*(Extends the roster in master_plan §2.4 with writing-facing detail. Every character also gets the standard brief: "the version of events in which they are the reasonable one.")*

- **Eliška Vranná / "Radek Mráz."** Wants: to be *sure* of one thing again. Mask: Radek — confident, transactional, funny in a way Eliška isn't. Voice: Eliška short, precise, questions as armor; Radek longer sentences, first-person plural ("we'll sort it"). The two registers are a deliverable of every dialogue file (blueprints §4.1). Sample (Eliška, journal): *"Third night without. I counted the stairs twice. Both counts were confident."*
- **Kapitán Doubek.** Wants: to retire having mattered. Mask: procedure. Voice: paragraphs that end in questions he already knows the answers to. Sample: *"You verified this. Good. And the part you didn't verify — walk me through why it's in the report."*
- **Dr. Sova.** Wants: to actually help one patient the system sent her. Mask: neutrality. Voice: soft, exact, never finishes the sentence that would be the diagnosis. Sample: *"You said 'he was there.' Earlier you said 'I saw him.' Those are different sentences. Which one do you want to keep?"*
- **Tereza Vranná.** Wants: her sister at Sunday lunch. Voice: unguarded, quick, the only person who interrupts Eliška. Sample: *"You sound tired in a new way. What's the new way?"*
- **Zdeněk Hora.** Wants: to be *survived by something orderly*. Mask: the tired king; the mask is that there is no mask left. Voice: aphoristic, unhurried, asks about your mother. Sample: *"Kamila sells certainty, Standa sells fear. I only ever sold patience. Sit down, Radek."*
- **Kamila Rohanová.** Wants: the archive, run like a utility. Mask: transparency ("I'll tell you exactly what I want" — true, and a technique). Voice: numbered lists in speech; jokes delivered deadpan at her own expense. Sample: *"Three things. Two you'll like. I'll start with the third."*
- **Standa Vrba.** Wants: to have been *chosen*, not merely used, by Hora. Mask: simplicity — plays dumber than he is, which is not very dumb at all. Voice: warm, physical, nicknames for everyone; goes quiet instead of angry. Sample: *"You did that clean. Old man asked about you twice this week. Don't make me look stupid, yeah?"*
- **Josef Sedlák.** Wants: to die before the archive is opened. Mask: harmless pensioner who "just keeps the filing straight." Voice: passive constructions, weather, and then one sentence that shows he's read your file. Sample: *"Rain again. My knee knew first. — Mráz, was it? From Ostrava? Strange, the Ostrava I remember had no Mráz family on Hlubinská."*
- **The recruit (Milan).** Wants: to be Radek. The game's cruelest mirror; his arc tracks the player's methods — he copies what you *do*, never what you say. Voice: too fast, borrowed phrases (yours, one mission later).

## 3. Gazetteer (slice + key full-game spaces)
- **The safehouse:** two rooms above a shuttered travel agency, Old Town edge. Hub. Moves in Act 3 (mission 10) to a cold-water flat in Zálesí — the downgrade is legible in every surface (art_direction §5: the color script darkens with it).
- **Argus HQ ("the House"):** a converted 1920s insurance building, Riverside. Day-phase social space; the archive floor (mission 5+) is below it — climate-controlled, card-keyed, the game's inner sanctum.
- **Club Litera:** the rival operator's club (mission 2), a nightclub back-house maze. Day/night variant pair.
- **The notary's office (Paper Weight, mission 4):** cramped verticality of paper; the slice's tensest sightline pockets.
- **Zálesí works ruins (mission 1):** the 1993 wreck as a physical place — the game opens inside the previous decade's corpse, deliberately.
- **Regional police depot (mission 7), Hora's villa (missions 6/10), the archive floor (missions 5/11/12):** full docs at their authoring passes.

## 4. Ground-Truth Outlines — Prologue + Slice Missions
*(Format per outline: truth events · NPC knowledge deltas · expected debrief claims · deck intent. These are the §6.2 pipeline's stage-1 documents for the slice; graybox follows from these.)*

### P — "Cold Open" (prologue)
- **Truth:** a controlled training exercise, three days before insertion: tail a volunteer "subject" through Old Town, observe a staged handoff, report to Doubek. Sova session before; Ground taught and practiced. During the tail, **one scripted `SubtitleDrift`** (a shop assistant's line) — disclosed immediately afterward in a mini-Theater with Sova narrating what happened and why it will happen again.
- **Knowledge:** Doubek learns Eliška's baseline honesty (the first report is compared against a fully observed exercise — diegetically justifying the debrief mechanic forever). Sova opens her file.
- **Debrief claims (expected):** 4–6, trivially checkable; one concerns the drifted line — filing it "as seen" is *correct behavior* and the debrief says so. The game's first lesson: honest error is not a sin here.
- **Deck:** the one scripted op only. Director inert.

### M1 — "Induction"
- **Truth:** night ride-along with Vrba's crew (driver + Milan) to collect a debt from a scrap dealer squatting the Zálesí works ruins. The dealer's nephew is present, armed, frightened — the encounter is authored to be resolvable clean (talk/intimidate via Radek), quiet (nonlethal), or loud (the nephew fires first *only if* alarmed). A body-in-the-dark set-piece: real, not phantom — establishing that the world's dread is usually true, so later doubt has a baseline.
- **Knowledge:** Vrba's crew forms Radek's first reputation entries (how clean, how calm). Doubek gets the op's existence but *not* Argus's take —the first thing Eliška can choose to under-report.
- **Debrief claims:** ~8; first real honesty-mode choice (did Radek strike the dealer? was the nephew armed?). No distortion-tainted claims yet — the moral weight arrives before the epistemic weight, by design.
- **Deck:** tier-1 audio only (`PhantomAudio` footsteps in the ruins, max 2 events). Budget floor regardless of mind state: this mission teaches *the world*, not the doubt.

### M2 — "The Listening Room"
- **Truth (day):** case Club Litera as Radek — three plant sites to identify, staff patterns to learn, one conversation with the club manager that seeds mission 4. **Truth (night):** re-enter closed club, plant two bugs, exit. The night layout *is* the day layout — the minimap is the player's daytime memory (master_plan §4.14), and this is the mission that teaches it.
- **Knowledge:** Rohanová (who ordered the job) gets her first read on Radek's precision. The club manager remembers whatever Radek's cover story claimed — interrupt-memory's first live wire.
- **Debrief claims:** ~10, including the first *perceived-but-wrong* candidate if the deck fired (a staffer the player "saw" return who didn't). First chance to file a Verified-only debrief.
- **Deck:** `SubtitleDrift` + `AudioSwap`, max 3 events; one authored `GeometrySwap` candidate (a corridor's remembered length) armed only at fatigue Murmur+.

### M3 — "Smoke Test" (slice climax)
- **Truth:** Argus stages a leak to test the new man: Radek is fed a false detail (a delivery time), and Vrba watches what surfaces. Day: a social gauntlet at the House where three people probe the detail differently. Night: the "delivery" — which is the test's live stage; the player's conduct (including an expected beating of a supposed informant, who is *also* a plant) is scored by hidden watchers. The beating is resolvable convincing-and-nonlethal with skill; walking away is possible and expensive; excess is possible and cheap. The mission is a mirror with a fist.
- **Knowledge:** pass → Vrba vouches for Radek (Act-1 gate); the fed detail's path proves Eliška *didn't* leak it — which is why Argus starts trusting Radek precisely when Doubek starts wondering how she's paying for that trust.
- **Debrief claims:** ~12, and the knife turns: reporting the fed detail to Doubek is *true, verified — and the test's tripwire* if filed carelessly (the game's first taste of claims-as-weapons; survivable either way, instructive both ways).
- **Deck:** full tier-1 set + first `PhantomEntity` (a watcher on a roofline — who is *sometimes real*: the deck contains both the phantom and a true watcher at different beats; the Theater afterward is the slice's showcase reveal).

## 5. Writing Standards
- Tone rules per master_plan §2.8; register rules per character (§2 above); Czech naming/idiom vetted per master_plan §6.3.
- Every mission's full ground-truth doc must answer, before dialogue is written: what does each present NPC truly know, want, and expect of Radek in each scene; what are the 3–5 claims this mission exists to make the player *have to characterize* in debrief.
- Word budgets: per master_plan §6.1 (slice ~60–80k). Marginalia and journal entries are written per mind-band (four variants where the band plausibly varies) — budgeted, not improvised.
